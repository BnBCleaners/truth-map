# QAnon

Decentralized political-conspiracy movement that emerged from anonymous imageboard posts in 2017. Core claim: Donald Trump wages a secret war against a global elite trafficking / “deep state” cabal; “Q” drops are framed as insider intelligence. The movement had real political and social effects. Many of its specific predictions failed.

**Media rule:** Text only. No graphic “proof” imagery.

## Origins (short spine)

| When | What |
|------|------|
| **28 Oct 2017** | First posts later tied to the “Q” persona appear on **4chan** |
| 2018+ | Activity concentrates on **8chan**; tripcodes used to signal continuity |
| Aug 2019 | 8chan goes offline |
| Nov 2019 | **8kun** successor; Q-branded posting continues in that ecosystem |
| Dec 2020 | Extended quiet after the election |
| Jun 2022 | Q-branded posts return; authorship continuity **Contested** |

Identity theories (e.g. linguistic attribution to early promoters or board operators) remain **Contested**; named parties have denied being Q.

---

## Trump (public behavior only)

- Movement cast Trump as central hero; he did not author drops (**Unsupported** that he did).
- **15 Oct 2020** NBC town hall: asked to denounce QAnon, he declined to clearly call the theory false, praised people who “fight” pedophilia, and used “I don’t know that and neither do you”–style deflection. Exchange is **True** (broadcast).
- Q communities widely treated that exchange as soft confirmation. Reception pattern **True**.
- Degree of private “strategic play” **Contested**; effect on the movement **Supported**.

## Continuity

After the Storm failed, the movement fragmented. Some influencers kept the cosmology without regular Q drops; some politicians dropped the brand while retaining overlapping themes. Motifs survived; a single continuous formal Q command structure is **Contested / Unsupported**.
