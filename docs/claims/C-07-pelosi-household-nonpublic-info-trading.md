# C-07 — Pelosi household trades using non-public information

**Claim ID:** C-07  
**Label:** Contested  
**Parent Case:** Congressional / Executive Stock Trading  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-31

---

**Plain-English claim:**  
Nancy Pelosi (or her husband Paul, who manages the household brokerage activity) makes well-timed stock and options trades because of access to non-public information from her official position.

---

## Evidence that supports the claim (or the appearance that fuels it)

- Official House Periodic Transaction Reports document large, repeated trades in major technology names (NVIDIA, Apple, Amazon, Alphabet, etc.), including options activity.  
- Public trackers that scrape the official filings highlight multi-year gains and well-timed entries/exits.  
- The household ranks among the most closely watched in Congress for disclosed trading activity.  
- A substantial copycat / tracker ecosystem exists (X accounts, copy-trading apps, NANC ETF) that treats the household’s disclosed activity as a signal worth following. This proves public interest and mirroring behavior; it does not prove non-public information use.  
- Proposed legislation to ban individual stock ownership by members and spouses has repeatedly cited high-profile examples including this household.

## Evidence that goes against or limits the claim

- No criminal conviction, SEC enforcement action, or formal STOCK Act violation finding specific to these trades has been located in the public record reviewed.  
- Paul Pelosi is described as managing the brokerage activity; spousal trading is disclosed jointly but is legally distinct in many analyses.  
- Abnormal or well-timed returns do not by themselves prove use of non-public information. Alternative explanations (skilled private management, public information, market beta) are not ruled out by the disclosure record alone.  
- STOCK Act disclosures exist precisely so the public can see the activity; visibility is not the same as proof of improper information flow.

## Primary missing link

Appearance evidence (disclosures, performance narratives, copycat ecosystem) is strong. What is still absent is a documented chain: *specific non-public information in the member’s possession → specific trade that depends on that information*. Until that chain exists in primary sources, the claim stays **Contested**.
