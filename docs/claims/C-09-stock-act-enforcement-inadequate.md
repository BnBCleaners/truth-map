# C-09 — STOCK Act enforcement is inadequate

**Claim ID:** C-09  
**Label:** True (on pattern of late filings / rare penalties) + Likely but Unproven (on the full “inadequate” policy verdict)  
**Parent Case:** Congressional / Executive Stock Trading  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

---

**Plain-English claim:**  
The STOCK Act’s disclosure and enforcement regime does not meaningfully deter or punish improper trading by members of Congress; late filings are common and formal penalties are rare.

---

## Documented pattern (True)

- The STOCK Act (2012) requires members of Congress and certain staff to report covered securities transactions within a short window (generally 45 days).  
- Public reporting and congressional ethics data show repeated instances of late filings across multiple members and years. The first late-filing fee is $200 (Ethics Committees, not SEC).  
- Formal public penalties (fines, referrals that produce published sanctions) remain rare relative to the volume of late or incomplete disclosures.  
- **SEC enforcement residual (True):** No public SEC civil enforcement action (complaint, settled order, or administrative proceeding) against a member of Congress for insider trading under the STOCK Act or Exchange Act §10(b) has been located. The highest-profile investigation (former Sen. Richard Burr, early-2020 sales) was closed by the SEC without action in January 2023 after DOJ had already closed its parallel inquiry without charges.

## 2026 legislative developments (True)

- **H.R. 7008 — Stop Insider Trading Act** (Rep. Steil): Passed House 22 Jul 2026 (231–198); received in Senate. Purchase ban for Members/spouses/dependents + 7–14 day sale-notice requirement + higher penalties. No forced divestiture.  
- **S. 1498 — HONEST Act** (Sen. Hawley): Stronger ownership + sale ban + divestiture requirement; covers President/VP as well; on Senate Legislative Calendar (reported Dec 2025).  
- Bipartisan Restore Trust in Congress package and additional bills remain active but earlier-stage.  
- **No ownership-ban bill has been enacted.**

## Full “inadequate” verdict

Whether the observed pattern of late filings + rare penalties + absence of SEC actions constitutes systemic enforcement failure that renders the Act effectively toothless is a policy judgment. On the current record the descriptive pattern is **True**; the stronger normative conclusion that enforcement is “inadequate” in a way that proves the law has failed is treated as **Likely but Unproven** pending fuller quantitative enforcement data and comparative benchmarks. The 2026 legislative activity is the most concrete response since 2012; it does not by itself change the current labels.
