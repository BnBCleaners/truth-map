# C-09 — STOCK Act enforcement is inadequate

**Claim ID:** C-09  
**Label:** True (on pattern of late filings / rare penalties) + Likely but Unproven (on the full “inadequate” policy verdict)  
**Parent Case:** Congressional / Executive Stock Trading  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-31

---

**Plain-English claim:**  
The STOCK Act’s disclosure and enforcement regime does not meaningfully deter or punish improper trading by members of Congress; late filings are common and formal penalties are rare.

---

## Documented pattern (True)

- The STOCK Act (2012) requires members of Congress and certain staff to report covered securities transactions within a short window (originally 30–45 days depending on the provision).  
- Public reporting and congressional ethics data show repeated instances of late filings across multiple members and years.  
- Formal public penalties (fines, referrals that produce published sanctions) remain rare relative to the volume of late or incomplete disclosures.  
- Multiple reform proposals (ownership bans, stronger automatic penalties, expanded coverage) have been introduced but have not been enacted into lasting, comprehensive ownership restrictions.

## Full “inadequate” verdict

Whether the observed pattern of late filings + rare penalties constitutes systemic enforcement failure that renders the Act effectively toothless is a policy judgment. On the current record the descriptive pattern is **True**; the stronger normative conclusion that enforcement is “inadequate” in a way that proves the law has failed is treated as **Likely but Unproven** pending fuller quantitative enforcement data and comparative benchmarks.
