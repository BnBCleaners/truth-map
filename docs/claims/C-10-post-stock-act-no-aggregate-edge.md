# C-10 — No reliable aggregate market-beating edge for Congress after STOCK Act

**Claim ID:** C-10  
**Label:** Likely but Unproven  
**Parent Case:** Congressional / Executive Stock Trading  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-31

---

**Plain-English claim:**  
Once the STOCK Act increased transparency and researchers apply proper market and sector controls, congressional (and household) portfolios as a group do not show a reliable, statistically significant market-beating edge attributable to non-public information.

---

## Supporting evidence

- Pre-STOCK Act studies (e.g., Ziobrowski) claimed substantial abnormal returns.  
- Post-STOCK Act work with improved data and controls (Belmont et al. 2022 on 2012–2020 PTRs; Chen & Sacerdote NBER w35041, 2026 on 2012–2023) finds portfolios that underperform or match benchmarks on average; upper-percentile results are consistent with random picking or public-signal following rather than systematic private-information advantage.  
- Timing analyses in the parent Case that controlled for public news calendars did not isolate clean non-public catalysts for the highest-visibility clusters examined.

## Limits

- Aggregate averages can mask individual outliers.  
- Spouse / trust trading attribution remains a measurement complication.  
- Academic findings are only as strong as their data windows and methodology; future primary-document revelations could shift the picture for specific members.

**Label:** The best current controlled studies lean against a reliable systemic edge → **Likely but Unproven**. This does not resolve C-07 or C-08 for any individual household; it constrains the systemic claim.
