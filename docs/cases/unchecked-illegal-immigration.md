# Case: Unchecked / Illegal Immigration — Scale, Drivers, Funding, and Intent

**Status:** Open · High public interest  
**Focus:** United States + Europe (post-2015 / post-2021 acceleration)  
**Completeness:** ~98% (Lev1 core)  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-31

---

## Scope

This Case examines large-scale unauthorized / illegal / irregular migration into high-income destination countries. It tracks official statistics, policy regimes, funding streams, political coalitions, and contested claims about intentionality.

**In scope:** Unauthorized entry, visa overstays that become illegal presence, asylum systems interacting with enforcement, facilitation networks, and funding trails that can be documented with primary sources.

**Out of scope (unless a specific Angle later requires them):** Ordinary legal immigration pathways and properly adjudicated refugee resettlement under the 1951 Refugee Convention.

Definitions follow primary legal and statistical sources (CBP, Frontex, UN DESA/IOM, national asylum authorities).

## 1. Scale — United States

### Annual Southwest Border encounters (CBP)

| Fiscal Year | Approx. encounters | Notes |
|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| FY2021 | ~1.7 million | Sharp post-2020 rise |
| FY2022 | ~2.2–2.4 million | Record high |
| FY2023 | ~2.0–2.5 million | Near-record; Title 42 ended May 2023 |
| FY2024 | ~1.5 million | Decline from peak |
| FY2025 | Sharp further decline | Border Patrol encounters at multi-decade lows |

Got-away estimates (DHS internal, publicly reported): roughly 389k (FY21), 600k (FY22), 670k (FY23). Cumulative known got-aways since FY2021 reported in the low millions.

### Monthly shape (key window)

- Rising through early 2023 into the 150k–180k range  
- Title 42 ended **11 May 2023** → June dip, then rebound  
- **December 2023 record: ~249,700** — highest single month in the modern series  
- Sustained decline through 2024  
- FY2025: often under 10,000 USBP encounters per month (historic lows)

**Definition note:** Encounters are events, not unique people. Series include Title 8 and (while active) Title 42.

### Origin composition (directional)

Pre-2021: Mexico + Northern Triangle dominant.  
Surge years: major expansion of Venezuela, Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua (CHNV) plus many extra-continental nationalities.  
Exact ranked multi-year citizenship tables remain available in the CBP Data Portal for precision work.

## 2. Scale — Europe (Frontex)

| Year | Irregular border-crossing detections | Notes |
|------|--------------------------------------|-------|
| 2023 | ~380,000 | Highest since 2016; Central Mediterranean ~41% |
| 2024 | ~239,000 | −38% |
| 2025 | ~178,000 | −26%; lowest since 2021 |

**Routes:** Central Mediterranean remained the most active even as totals fell. Western Balkans and Western Africa saw strong declines; Western Mediterranean occasionally increased.

**2025 top detected nationalities (Frontex public statements):** Bangladeshis, Egyptians, Afghans. Earlier peak years featured Syria, Guinea, Côte d'Ivoire prominently.

## 3. Key U.S. policy levers

| Policy | Facts |
|--------|-------|
| **Title 42** | Active Mar 2020 – 11 May 2023. ~3 million expulsions over its life |
| **Title 42 end** | Full shift to Title 8 processing |
| **CBP One** | >900,000 successful appointments by late 2024 |
| **CHNV parole** | Cubans 110,240 · Haitians 211,040 · Nicaraguans 93,080 · Venezuelans 117,320 · **Total ~531,700** (late 2024 cumulative). Between-ports encounters of these nationalities fell ~91%. Program terminated early 2025 |

CHNV monthly trajectory: rapid 2023 ramp toward the 30,000/month design rate → high throughput mid/late 2023 → clear decline through 2024 → termination 2025.

## 4. Funding (U.S. primary pass)

**Shelter and Services Program (SSP)** — DHS/FEMA:

| FY | Amount | Recipients |
|----|--------|------------|
| 2023 | $363.8 million awarded | 56 entities (state/local + NGOs; nearly half to NGOs) |
| 2024 | $640.9 million available | Allocation + competitive rounds |

Documented high-value recipients include Catholic Charities (Diocese of San Diego), Pima County AZ, Maricopa County AZ, El Paso County TX, Riverside County CA, Yuma area, and City/County of Denver.

**Label:** Scale and stated purpose (post-release shelter, food, transport, medical care) = Confirmed. Claims that this funding *caused* increased illegal crossings = Contested.

## 6. Political coalitions & litigation

### Who pushed which direction (U.S.)

| Side | Actors / tools |
|------|----------------|
| **Higher enforcement** | Trump 2nd-term executive posture; Republican border bills (e.g. H.R. 2); Texas and other state measures; FAIR, CIS, NumbersUSA, IRLI |
| **Lower enforcement intensity / opposition to restrictions** | Biden-era policy mix (Title 42 end, CHNV, SSP, CBP One); Democratic opposition to strict packages; sanctuary jurisdictions; ACLU affiliates, American Immigration Council, immigrant-rights NGOs |

### Litigation was used by both sides

- Texas (+ Missouri) sued to force reinstatement of Remain in Mexico (MPP)  
- Federal government sued Texas over buoys and razor wire  
- ACLU + partners sued the Biden administration over post-Title 42 asylum limits and the June 2024 restrictions  
- Earlier blocks of Trump-era asylum/entry rules  

Courts functioned as a central veto player in both directions.

## 8. Assessment

**Confirmed**  
Large unauthorized flows into the U.S. and Europe are documented in official statistics. Policy choices (Title 42 end, parole programs, release practices, funding for post-release services) had measurable effects on volumes and composition. Political coalitions consistently pushed opposite directions of enforcement intensity. Funding relationships between governments and service/advocacy organizations exist and can be traced.

**Contested**  
Claims that the unauthorized surge was the *intended objective* of policy rather than a tolerated or under-estimated byproduct. Claims of centralized foreign-adversary direction of the flows. Strength of any causal link from specific funding streams to higher illegal entries.

**Unsupported at this stage**  
Any blanket assertion that the entire phenomenon was either purely spontaneous or purely orchestrated.
